BSc Thesis · Completed Sep 2026

🌊 Design and Performance Analysis of an Intelligent Sensor and Locally Hosted Web-Based IoT Framework for Integrated Aquaponics Farming

Islamic University of Technology — Department of EEE
Authors: Alusine Bah, Abdoulatif Mhadjou, Bello Aminu Ado
Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Khondokar Habibul Kabir

Abstract. Food insecurity in Sub-Saharan Africa is worsened by reliance on traditional farming, which is sensitive to soil, rainfall and manual oversight. Aquaponics combines fish farming with soilless crop growth in a closed loop, but its biological complexity demands continuous monitoring and automatic control. This thesis presents the design, construction and evaluation of an intelligent sensor-based IoT framework for integrated aquaponics farming, built on a hybrid Arduino Mega and ESP32 architecture.

The Mega performs all control decisions locally using threshold-based hysteresis, while the ESP32 hosts the Smart Aquaponics Operating System (SAOS) dashboard for monitoring, manual override, sensor diagnostics and REST access. The prototype is solar-powered and was tested with live fish and plants across seventeen measured parameters and ten actuators.

Key results. Performance was evaluated in terms of communication latency, packet delivery ratio, system response time and energy consumption:

  • Communication latency: 2 ms mean (σ = 1.25 ms, N = 2000 packets)
  • Packet delivery ratio: 93% over a 30-minute window
  • System response time: 0.029 s mean (local decision path)
  • Energy consumption: 17.2 W nominal · 26.9 W peak (solar-powered)

The results show that a reliable, locally controlled aquaponics system can be built from commercially available components and is a practical option for small-scale farming in resource-limited regions.

Public Health Research

Community Research · Njala University

🏥 Knowledge, Attitude & Practice of Self-Medication Among Youth in Rokupr, Kambia District, Sierra Leone

Njala University — Department of Community Health & Clinical Studies
Author: Alusine Bah (Fadda Bah)
Study Area: Rokupr Town, Magbema Chiefdom, Kambia District, Sierra Leone

Background. Self-medication — the use of medicines without medical supervision — is a growing component of healthcare in both developing and developed countries. Unlike other aspects of self-care, it involves drugs that carry the potential to do both good and harm. In Sierra Leone, drugs are freely displayed for sale in unauthorized places such as markets, shops, and roadside stalls, and reported prevalence of self-medication ranges between 15.0% and 81.5% across different localities.

Objective. To assess the knowledge, attitude, and practice of self-medication among youth in Rokupr Town, and to identify its health impact and propose actionable solutions to reduce the practice.

Specific objectives:

  • Examine the knowledge of youth on self-medication in the study area
  • Assess the attitude of youth towards self-medication
  • Identify the impact of self-medication practices on youth in Rokupr
  • Proffer solutions to eradicate self-medication practices among youth

Methodology. A cross-sectional descriptive study using both qualitative and quantitative methods. Data was collected via structured interview questionnaires and focus group discussions (FGDs) across five randomly selected clusters of secondary schools in Rokupr Town. A sample size of 182 respondents aged 10–45 was calculated using a 95% confidence interval and a 5% degree of accuracy. Data was analyzed with SPSS and Microsoft Excel.

Theoretical framework. Grounded in Dorothea Orem's Self-Care Theory (1971), which frames self-medication as a deliberate self-care activity to maintain health, and which shows that the practice arises from the same behavioral drivers as everyday self-care.

Expected contribution. The findings will inform government ministries, health institutions, humanitarian organizations, and future researchers working to curb self-medication among youth in Sierra Leone and comparable settings. The study is a partial fulfillment of the Higher National Diploma in Community Health (CHO) at Njala University.
