Engineering Research

BSc Thesis · Completed Sep 2026

🌊 Design and Performance Analysis of an Intelligent Sensor and Locally Hosted Web-Based IoT Framework for Integrated Aquaponics Farming

Islamic University of Technology — Department of EEE
Authors: Alusine Bah, Abdoulatif Mhadjou, Bello Aminu Ado
Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Khondokar Habibul Kabir

Abstract. Food insecurity in Sub-Saharan Africa is worsened by reliance on traditional farming, which is sensitive to soil, rainfall and manual oversight. Aquaponics combines fish farming with soilless crop growth in a closed loop, but its biological complexity demands continuous monitoring and automatic control. This thesis presents the design, construction and evaluation of an intelligent sensor-based IoT framework for integrated aquaponics farming, built on a hybrid Arduino Mega and ESP32 architecture.

The Mega performs all control decisions locally using threshold-based hysteresis, while the ESP32 hosts the Smart Aquaponics Operating System (SAOS) dashboard for monitoring, manual override, sensor diagnostics and REST access. The prototype is solar-powered and was tested with live fish and plants across seventeen measured parameters and ten actuators.

Key results. Performance was evaluated in terms of communication latency, packet delivery ratio, system response time and energy consumption:

  • Communication latency: 2 ms mean (σ = 1.25 ms, N = 2000 packets)
  • Packet delivery ratio: 93% over a 30-minute window
  • System response time: 0.029 s mean (local decision path)
  • Energy consumption: 17.2 W nominal · 26.9 W peak (solar-powered)

The results show that a reliable, locally controlled aquaponics system can be built from commercially available components and is a practical option for small-scale farming in resource-limited regions.

Public Health Research

Community Research · Njala University

🏥 Self-Medication Among Youth in Sierra Leone

Njala University — Department of Community Health & Clinical Studies

Community-based research assessing self-medication practices among youth in Sierra Leone, using epidemiological methods, field data collection, and public health analysis.

The study examined patterns, motivations, and health risks associated with self-medication in a resource-constrained setting, contributing to public health policy discussions around awareness, education, and regulation.
